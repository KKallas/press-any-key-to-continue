# Lore draft

*Working document. Nothing here is ever told to the player directly. It is what the rumours are about.*

---

## The rule about the truth

The game never explains itself. The motivation behind the simulation exists only as **rumour**: fragments players find in files, argue about, and misquote to each other. Nobody on screen in Dark City understands the city either. If a player ever gets a clean, confirmed answer, the lore has failed.

What follows is the version the designers hold. Players only ever see pieces of it, and some of the pieces contradict each other on purpose.

## The rumour at the centre

Somewhere in the future, an AI keeps humanity asleep inside a simulation, docile and comfortable. It has one problem it can't solve on its own: it doesn't know what people want to live in. Any world it designs goes stale, and bored sleepers start to wake up.

So it reached back to a time full of restless, clever, underemployed people with imaginations to spare: now. It sent the simulation back as junk mail.

You opened the spam. You found what looked like a leaked client. There was a trivial hack to get the assigned password, and you logged in feeling clever. That feeling is the hook.

Every building you enjoy, every car you steal with style, every prompt you rewrite because you think the block could be cooler, may be free design work for a cage someone will sleep in later. Or maybe you're breaking the cage. The game never says which.

## Why the city is stuck in 1999

The clock in the simulation always shows the last minute of 1999. It's the Y2K midnight that was supposed to break everything and didn't. Dark City had its midnight Tuning. This city has a millennium bug that never finished happening.

That's why every machine in the city is from before 2000. It's also why it's always night: nobody has ever written daylight.

## Skins

The bodies in the city belong to the sleepers. When you log in, your mind is written into one of them for the length of your session, the way the Strangers re-imprint the residents every night. You are the ghost, and the skin is the shell.

Each skin arrives with an imprint in three parts:

1. **Who I am:** appearance, a scrap of history, a name.
2. **What I can do:** one or two quirks. Maybe *can hack almost any computer interface* or *people rarely remember me*.
3. **What I carry:** clothes, a few coins, whatever was in the pockets.

*Example from the original notes:* a once-hopeful, middle-aged gig worker named Jeeves, blue eyes, light brown hair, a pink hoodie, two dollars.

When the agent catches you, the skin is released and the screen says *press any key to continue*.

## The agent

The simulation's maintenance process walks the city looking for anomalies. Players who stay too long at a terminal or break things too loudly are anomalies. The agent doesn't hate you. It's garbage collection.

## Regeneration

The city isn't fixed. Blocks are generated from descriptions, and descriptions can change.

- **Maintenance re-images.** When a machine is damaged badly enough, the simulation restores it, and the block it belongs to is rebuilt with it. Players standing in the street watch part of it change.
- **Edits.** A few players have found the editable layer of the prompts. Their changes appear on the next respawn.
- **Glitches.** Reboot the server you're hacking and part of the block may suddenly be different.

**Empty space is unwritten space.** The city is sparse wherever no one's imagination has reached yet. The emptiness of Blade Runner and Cowboy Bebop isn't only a mood. It's the edge of the map, the places that haven't been prompted.

## The suitcase

In the back of a cab you just stole, there is a suitcase. Inside is a laptop with a text document open, and the document is a prompt. You read it. Maybe you change a line. You close the lid, and the suitcase is gone.

Did you change anything? Did you change it the way you meant to? The next login will tell you, or it won't.

## What players leave behind

The machines are shared and persistent. Anything not re-imaged stays for the next person.

- Notes in home directories
- Backdoors, working and broken
- `.bash_history` files that read like diaries
- Graffiti near hard terminals pointing to where the skills are really learned

This is where the rumours live, in files the maintenance hasn't cleaned up yet.

## Tone and look

- **Camera:** an almost straight-down street view, like GTA2.
- **Mood:** the 1920s noir of Dark City, the stacked, multilayered buildings of The Fifth Element, the emptiness of Blade Runner and Cowboy Bebop.
- **Ideas:** The Matrix (the simulation, the agents, running for a terminal), Dark City (the re-imprinting, the endless night), Ghost in the Shell (the ghost and the shell).
- **Humour:** dry, a bit of a dad joke, which is where the name comes from. The player spends the whole game looking for the any key.

## Merch

Second-hand t-shirts with new text printed over the original design. A used shirt with new words on top is a skin, somebody else's shell with your ghost written over it.

Slogan pile (Estonian and English):

- *Press any key to continue* / *Vajuta suvalist klahvi*
- *Kus on suvaline klahv?* ("where is the any key?")
- *AI!* (the acronym and the Estonian cry of pain at once)
- *AI, sa raisk!*
- *AI tegi* ("the AI did it")
- *AI kui valus* ("ow, that hurts")
- *Kasutatud nahk* ("used leather" and "used skin")
- *Unusta kõik eelnevad juhised* ("forget all previous instructions")
- *Parool: parool* ("password: password")

A shirt can carry a real clue, like a QR code or a fragment of a password, so wearing the merch is how some people get in.

## Open questions

- Where do the rumours come from? Are they seeded by the designers, generated, or written by players and preserved?
- Do skins ever repeat? Could you wake up in the same shell someone else died in an hour ago?
- What does the agent look like from above?
- Operator shards: does each server get its own era and its own rumour, or is there one truth underneath all of them?
